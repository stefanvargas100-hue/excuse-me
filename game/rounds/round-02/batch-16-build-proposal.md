# Build balance proposal — approval required

Recommend a single build-row adjustment: **large speed +4, acceleration -3, recovery -1** (currently +3/-4/+1). Keep small -3/+4/-1 and medium 0/0/0. This preserves zero-sum points, buys a slightly clearer cruise role and reduces sluggish starts at the cost of recovery. Director approval and human timing are required before production implementation. Nothing in production changed.

## Evidence and method

Source baseline: 2b66b8b. Run `node tools/build-balance.cjs`; seed 42, dt 1/60. Exact 36-case output: [measurements](batch-16-evidence/measurements.json); readable [table](batch-16-evidence/table.md). Both bodies, all three builds, and three identical-within-comparison kits are explicit in each row. Plain is starter gear; speed is suit/joggers/runners/cap/pack; responsive is tank/shorts/slides/cap/pack. Real fit bonuses and +/-10 caps apply. Candidate rows modify only the in-memory large definition before stats calculation, never source files.

The harness runs actual Classic ticks and burst actions on level 0. Synthetic setup: speed starts at zero, energy at 100, chain zero; crowd, bots, pickups and dynamic spawns are absent, timers/time-left are extended, position resets only BETWEEN isolated cases. No per-frame teleporting. Classic uses the same build acceleration, stamina pace and lateral rules as races; belts, strips, route multipliers, drafting, powerups and combo benefits are deliberately excluded. This is mechanics evidence, not complete race performance. Bump case begins at cruise and applies the real recovery timer, with no boosts/chain, then measures time and distance from timer application to 95% cruise; contact geometry and hit-stop are not modeled. Steering holds right physically from rest until 90% lateral maximum, before reaching a wall.

Each open run lasts 60s and reports distance at 2s/60s, target/actual cruise, t90/t99 and seconds above 99% cruise. Refill starts at zero and measures 0–100 without actions. Burst case lasts 120s, starting full, triggering actual burst only when energy >=70 and previous burst expired; duty is active ticks/7200, with observed minimum energy and average speed. The 70 threshold can spend down to 40; this policy intentionally avoids low-energy slowdown and is not an optimal-input claim. No dash expenditure in that case.

Validation: `node --test test/build-balance.test.js` passes 2/2. Known first tick is 3*1.012*3.5/60 = 0.1771; t90 uses the discrete recurrence (not continuous approximation); cruise and refill agree with constants to one tick. The initial placeholder failed the numeric assertion before implementation. Repeatability compares every returned field across two independent seed-42 runs; cross-VM object prototypes initially required returning plain host objects, after which comparison passes. Measurement-only scope: no full gameplay suite/build run, as permitted by this batch. `git diff --check` passes.

## Findings and tradeoffs

Plain male small/medium/large cruise is 2.982/3.036/3.090 m/s; t90 is .533/.650/.817s; 2s distance 5.304/5.256/5.131m, 60s distance 178.260/181.343/184.348m. Large wins prolonged clear walking but loses the start. Its advantage over small is about 3.62% speed against 33% less acceleration and 21.4% less lateral acceleration (4.2 versus 2.8 forward acceleration; lateral multipliers 1.12 versus .88). Female cruise is .072 m/s lower for each plain build, with faster recovery. Fixed hitbox radius is .25m; large's drawn bulk does not buy collision resistance.

Plain male bump-to-95% takes 2.000/2.083/2.250s and covers 3.028/3.315/3.816m. The longer distance is distance traveled during recovery, not a benefit or a lost-distance estimate. Steering90 takes .283/.317/.367s. Full refill takes 12.633/12.083/11.583s; burst duty .247/.256/.266 and mean speed 3.546/3.609/3.658 m/s. Large's modest recovery advantage does not erase its acceleration cost. Deceleration uses shared rate 5, while accelerating uses build stats. Near misses, combos and draft could outweigh these differences in real traffic.

Important cap weakness: male speed kit medium and large both cruise at 3.180 m/s, yet large covers 189.642m versus medium 189.897m in 60s due to slower acceleration. Equal build point sums do not protect a useful role under gear caps. Responsive kit also caps small acceleration: medium slightly beats small at 2s. These are loadout interactions, not grounds to silently retune gear or the cap.

Candidate plain male large cruise rises to 3.108, t90 improves to .767s, 2s distance to 5.225m and 60s to 185.487m. Steering90 improves to .350s. Bump95 remains 2.250s because the longer recovery timer offsets faster acceleration; distance falls to 3.737m. Refill worsens to 12.633s and burst mean falls to 3.647m/s. Female results show the same direction. Thus this is a clearer walking/start tradeoff, not an across-the-board buff. Male speed-kit large remains capped and behind medium over 60s (189.717 versus 189.897m); disclose that limitation. Small remains most responsive in plain gear, medium retains a start advantage over large and recovery advantage over small. Candidate responsive gear can beat medium on short distance: check that kit with humans before acceptance.

Conservative alternative: large +4/-4/0, leaving its acceleration/steering penalties intact and paying one recovery point for cruise. This alternative is an inference from the formulas, NOT simulated evidence; run the same tool and human protocol before selecting it. Neither proposal establishes competitive parity or eliminates cap-induced weak loadouts.

## Human timing and release gate

No crowded/shortcut controller attempt was made: the existing collision-heavy route-review controller is not competent-human evidence. Keep this limitation explicit rather than deriving full-race claims. On real phones, use current and approved-candidate builds in counterbalanced order, each body and the three kits above, same recorded seeds 4/8/42. Time a clear 20m start and 200m cruise, one standardized unavoidable bump then 20m recovery, crowded main route, and Subway main versus mezzanine (same entrance/exit markers, physical steering only). Run at least three paired repetitions per build/seed with two competent players; log splits, bumps, action counts, stamina lows, time near cruise and completion failures. Preserve failed runs, separate route choice from traversal skill, compare within-player medians/spread and record phone/input/frame rate. Check large is useful in clear/long segments, small responsive in traffic, and medium competitive with capped gear; do not approve if one build dominates every paired task. Browser emulation is not phone feel.

Future tuning affects both player and bot loadouts in races, and shared movement in Classic/Daily/Weekly plus applicable Practice/Challenge/Cup/Friend Cup/elimination setups. Recheck supplied outfits and medals rather than silently changing them. Version planning must cover Classic cm2 records/ghosts/boards, Daily/Weekly mp2 identities and period boards, race bests/ghosts via recordKey + courseTag, and race boards currently named race:<stage> without a rules suffix. RULES_VERSION=1 alone is insufficient: recordKey treats the current standard version as an unsuffixed legacy key, so simply incrementing that constant will still reuse standard keys. Add an explicit compatibility design for preserving old records and selecting separate new ones; align ghost read/write and board submit/read paths. Challenge has its own rules/board identity; assess its fixed outfit and bump its identity if affected. Cup/Friend Cup/Practice/Challenge suppress ordinary records by policy; elimination suppresses race records/ghosts/boards, but fair mixed-client competition still requires compatible rules. No IDs changed here. This study delays final medal calibration, competitive sign-off and release until Director approval, compatibility work, and human validation; it can proceed independently of shortcut content implementation.
